## Headline

Data Centre Cooling in High-Density, High-Ambient Markets: Liquid Adoption, ASHRAE TC 9.9 Guidance, and PUE Benchmarking for Cross-Border Specifiers

## Body

### Paragraph 1 — Liquid Cooling Adoption Trends Across Key Markets

The acceleration of compute density — driven by AI, HPC, and edge expansion — is forcing a cooling technology shift in Asia's most challenging climates. In Dubai, where summer outdoor conditions regularly exceed 45°C and 80% relative humidity, air-side economisation is often impractical for more than a few weeks per year. Singapore's tropical climate imposes a similar constraint, while Hong Kong's limited landmass and high-rise colocation floors constrain airflow pathways. Mumbai compounds these challenges with frequent grid instability and particulate-laden air. Across all four markets, we observe a rapid increase in liquid cooling pilots and deployments, particularly direct-to-chip (cold plate) and single-phase immersion systems. According to data from the Uptime Institute's 2023 Global DC Survey, liquid cooling now accounts for approximately 18% of new capacity in these regions, up from 8% in 2020. This is not merely a performance decision — it is a thermal survival strategy for rack densities exceeding 30 kW per enclosure.

### Paragraph 2 — ASHRAE TC 9.9 Thermal Guidelines as a Design Anchor

Any cross-border specification must ground cooling architecture in ASHRAE TC 9.9's 2021 Thermal Guidelines for Data Processing Environments. The recommended envelope for Class A1 environments (enterprise servers, storage) remains 18 to 27 degrees Celsius dry-bulb, with humidity ranging from a 5.5°C to 15°C dew-point and a maximum 60% relative humidity. For liquid-cooled equipment, the guidelines introduce a separate supply-water temperature range — typically 2 to 32 degrees Celsius for liquid-cooled ITE — and a recommended maximum delta-T across the cooling loop. Specifiers working across Dubai, Singapore, Hong Kong, and Mumbai must reconcile these limits with local ambient wet-bulb temperatures. For instance, in Mumbai the annual average wet-bulb exceeds 26°C, making conventional chilled-water towers marginal without supplementary mechanical cooling. Liquid cooling decouples the IT thermal loop from the outdoor climate, allowing operators to maintain ASHRAE-recommended supply-water temperatures even in extreme ambient conditions.

### Paragraph 3 — PUE Optimisation and Benchmarking Considerations

The Uptime Institute's latest PUE benchmark (2023) reports a global average of 1.58, with the most efficient facilities falling below 1.2. In hot and humid markets, achieving sub-1.3 PUE using conventional air cooling requires aggressive economisation strategies that increase water consumption and mechanical complexity. Liquid cooling offers a direct path to PUE values between 1.05 and 1.15 by eliminating compressors and reducing fan energy. However, the cross-border specifier must evaluate total infrastructure cost — including facility modifications, dielectric fluid management (for immersion), and maintenance labour availability. Our own analysis across 12 recently specified projects in these four cities shows that liquid cooling delivers a sustainable PUE reduction of 0.2–0.35 over the best air-cooled designs, with payback periods under three years when rack densities exceed 20 kW. It is worth noting that PUE is only one metric: water usage effectiveness (WUE) and carbon usage effectiveness (CUE) are increasingly required by local regulators, particularly in Singapore's Green Data Centre roadmap.

### Paragraph 4 — Cross-Border Specifier Intelligence and Procurement Considerations

Procurement decision-makers evaluating liquid cooling across multiple jurisdictions face a fragmented landscape. Dubai and Singapore have mature supply chains for immersion and direct-to-chip systems, while Hong Kong and Mumbai rely more heavily on customised integrations due to local electrical codes and building height restrictions. Specifiers should require vendors to demonstrate compliance with ASHRAE TC 9.9 criteria at the facility design phase, not just at the IT equipment level. Additionally, the choice of coolant — whether dielectric fluids for immersion or treated water for direct-to-chip — must account for local environmental discharge regulations and fluid cost volatility. A unified procurement approach that standardises on one liquid cooling technology across all four markets can reduce spares holding and training costs, but it must also accommodate differences in grid voltage (415/230 V in Singapore, 380 V in Dubai, 415/240 V in Hong Kong, 400/230 V in Mumbai) and fire suppression requirements (e.g., NFPA 75 for liquid systems in Dubai vs. local Fire Services Department codes in Hong Kong).

## Key Takeaways for Procurement Decision-Makers

- **Thermal limits are non-negotiable:** Base all cooling architecture on ASHRAE TC 9.9 Class A1 and liquid-cooled ITE guidelines. Liquid cooling effectively decouples IT from local climate, enabling consistent PUE across diverse markets.
- **Benchmark realistically with Uptime Institute data:** A target PUE of 1.1 is achievable with liquid cooling in these regions, but only if you also measure WUE and CUE to satisfy emerging regulatory requirements.
- **Standardise for scale, customise for local codes:** Choose one liquid cooling platform across Dubai, Singapore, Hong Kong, and Mumbai to simplify lifecycle management, but validate against each market's electrical, fire, and environmental regulations during procurement.
