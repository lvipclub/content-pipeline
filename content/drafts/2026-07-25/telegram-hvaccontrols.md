## Hook — Market Intelligence Angle

Data centre cooling is no longer a one-size-fits-all decision. From the arid heat of Dubai to the dense urban centres of Singapore, Hong Kong, and Mumbai, facility managers and specifiers face diverging drivers — power costs, land constraints, and sustainability mandates. Understanding cross-border trends in liquid cooling and air management is now essential for optimising PUE and future-proofing infrastructure.

## Body — Analytical Insight

Across these regions, PUE targets are tightening. In Dubai, with ambient temperatures exceeding 45°C, traditional CRAC and CRAH units struggle to maintain ASHRAE TC 9.9 recommended ranges (18°C–27°C inlet), pushing operators toward liquid cooling solutions such as direct-to-chip and rear-door heat exchangers. In Singapore, the Infocomm Media Development Authority (IMDA) sets a PUE goal of 1.2 for new data centres, driving adoption of hot/cold aisle containment combined with liquid-assisted cooling. Hong Kong, constrained by limited floor loading and high electricity tariffs, increasingly adopts high-density liquid cooling to reduce CRAC fan energy by up to 40%. Mumbai's rapid growth, coupled with frequent power grid fluctuations, demands resilient architectures — often a hybrid of chilled-water CRAH units with aisle containment and localised liquid loops.

Standards such as ASHRAE 90.4 (Energy Standard for Data Centres) and the EU Code of Conduct for Data Centre Energy Efficiency provide benchmarks. For example, a well-implemented hot aisle containment system can lower cooling energy by 15–30%, while a move to liquid cooling can reduce facility-level PUE by 0.1–0.3 compared with air-only strategies. Yet adoption varies widely: in Dubai, liquid cooling is still nascent outside hyperscale facilities; in Singapore, it is becoming mainstream for new builds; Hong Kong retrofits struggle with containment due to existing floor layouts; Mumbai typically uses a hybrid approach with evaporative pre-cooling on the air side.

## Key Takeaway

There is no universal cooling solution. The best path depends on your region's climate, grid reliability, and density demands. Start with robust aisle containment, then evaluate liquid cooling for high-density zones. Always model partial-load PUE and consider the total cost of ownership over a 10-year horizon.
