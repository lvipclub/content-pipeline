**Authority Before Cv: The Valve Selection Detail That Decides Control Quality**

Chilled-water systems are specified with real engineering — then quietly undone by one line in the valve schedule: an oversized control valve chosen for margin rather than authority.

Valve authority is the valve's share of the circuit pressure drop at full flow. It is the number that decides whether the characteristic you selected in the catalogue still exists once the valve is installed. The widely used benchmark is an authority of about 0.5; fall well below it and the installed characteristic collapses toward quick-opening behaviour — the valve spends its working life at small lifts, and the loop hunts at part load [ASHRAE Handbook—Fundamentals].

The pattern is familiar on site:

1. **Oversizing is the silent failure.** Safety margins compound — coil selection, then valve sizing on top — and the valve ends up an on/off device in a modulating role. The symptom shows up as a low-delta-T plant problem; the cause is a selection problem.

2. **Equal-percentage exists to pair with coils.** Coil heat output is fast at high flow and slow at low flow. Equal-percentage mirrors that shape — but only while authority holds. On oversized valves the pairing never gets a chance to work.

3. **Pressure-independent packages are the specifier's lever.** They hold flow against varying differential pressure — buying back authority at the cost of extra circuit pressure drop. On variable-flow loops with wide DP swings, that trade is often worth making.

The discipline is unglamorous but cheap: size for authority first, verify the full-load circuit pressures, and treat generous Cv margin as a control-quality risk, not a comfort.
