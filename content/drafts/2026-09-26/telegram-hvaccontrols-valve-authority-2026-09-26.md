## Post (target ≤ 1024 — sendPhoto hard limit; no emoji)

An oversized control valve doesn't control — it switches.

Four things to hold the line on, water-side:

1. Authority beats Cv. A valve's share of the circuit pressure drop at full flow decides whether the selected characteristic survives installation. Around 0.5 is the widely used benchmark; well below that, the installed curve collapses toward quick-opening.

2. Oversizing is the silent failure. Generous safety margins park the valve at small lifts — hunting loops, overshoot at part load, and a low-delta-T pattern that reads as a plant fault when it is a selection fault.

3. Equal-percentage exists to pair with coils. Coil output is fast at high flow, slow at low flow — equal-percentage mirrors that, but only while authority holds.

4. Pressure-independent packages buy back authority at the cost of extra circuit pressure drop — worth weighing on wide-DP variable-flow loops.
