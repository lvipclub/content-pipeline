**Free Cooling Is a Controls Decision, Not a Damper Installation**

Air-side economisers are one of the few efficiency measures codes effectively mandate: ASHRAE 90.1 requires them across most climate zones, with climate-dependent high-limit cutouts [ANSI/ASHRAE/IES 90.1]. The hardware arrives on the drawing. What arrives unevenly is the operating discipline — and free cooling is banked, or lost, at changeover, hour by hour.

Three failure patterns decide the outcome.

Changeover on the wrong variable. Dry-bulb changeover asks "is the outdoor air cool?" when the operating question is "is it cool AND dry?" In humid coastal markets — which covers most of Asia's coastal CBDs — a 22°C morning can carry a dew point high enough that admitting the air adds latent duty the coil must then remove, and the chiller runs anyway. Differential enthalpy or dew-point changeover is the honest test in these climates.

Sensing drift. Differential enthalpy compares outdoor air with return air, so the decision is only as good as two sensors. A drifted element does not fail loudly; it quietly flips the changeover decision every day. Sensor calibration belongs inside the energy conservation measure, not on a maintenance wish-list.

The silent mechanical failure. A damper seized at minimum position raises no alarm and files no complaint — it simply removes the free-cooling hours from the year. Trending the outdoor-air damper signal against mixed-air temperature catches it; this is exactly the class of anomaly analytics platforms surface when trend data is clean [XINCA Knowledge Base: ai-building-energy-management].

For the next performance brief, one line carries most of the value: the changeover type, the high-limit setpoint, and who verifies the two sensors. ASHRAE Guideline 36's advanced sequences formalise much of this discipline [XINCA Knowledge Base: ASHRAE Guideline 36] — the rest is commissioning honesty.
