# Topic: IAQ — CO₂ monitoring accuracy, sensor placement, and demand-controlled ventilation
# Date: Mon 03 Aug 2026

**Audience:** Specifiers, consultants, facility managers — market intelligence voice

**Hook:**

CO₂ sensors are the fastest-growing line item in commercial ventilation specifications across Asia and the Gulf — and the most inconsistently specified. Two sensors in the same room routinely disagree by 200-300 ppm, and the cause is rarely the sensor itself. It is placement, calibration assumptions, and a spec that never says where the sensor goes or how it gets verified.

Three specification decisions decide whether your CO₂-based demand-controlled ventilation works for a decade or drifts into fiction:

1. **NDIR with self-calibration assumes fresh air.** Automatic baseline correction (ABC) recalibrates against the assumption that a space periodically sees near-outdoor air (~400 ppm). A meeting room that never sees fresh air drifts high over months — and the BMS then over-ventilates quietly, burning fan and thermal energy [ASHRAE 62.1].

2. **Placement is a spec item, not a site decision.** Sensor beside a supply diffuser reads diluted supply air. Sensor over a doorway reads the corridor. The 62.1 guidance points at the breathing zone or a return airstream that genuinely represents the occupied zone average — write that into the specification instead of leaving it to the installer [ASHRAE 62.1-2022, Informative Appendix].

3. **Verification is cheap; recalibration is not.** A reference-gas check at commissioning, then at the manufacturer's interval, catches drift before it becomes a comfort complaint. Budget it, or the DCV energy savings silently disappear into over-ventilation.

The honest caveat for your next brief: CO₂ is a proxy for people, not a universal IAQ gauge. It tracks occupancy-driven ventilation loads; it will not see VOC off-gassing from fit-out materials or PM2.5 ingress. Pair CO₂-based DCV with the ventilation rates that match the space's actual pollutants [ASHRAE 62.1].

What this means for your next spec: sensor accuracy (±50 ppm + 5% of reading, NDIR), placement per the breathing-zone rule, a commissioning baseline, and a recalibration interval — all in writing. The difference between a DCV project that saves outdoor-air energy and one that argues about readings for years is four lines in the specification.
