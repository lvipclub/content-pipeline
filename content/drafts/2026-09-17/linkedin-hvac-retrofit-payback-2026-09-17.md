## Post (within LinkedIn's 3,000-char post limit)

**The Retrofit Question Every Asset Owner Will Face This Decade**

Every ageing plant eventually reaches the boardroom: replace the chiller plant, or squeeze another decade from it. The honest answer starts upstream of the equipment. Most plants do not underperform because the hardware is tired — they underperform because the control sequences were never commissioned to the design intent. Schedules that ignore occupancy, missing reset strategies, deadbands too tight to matter, economisers that never change over cleanly. The plant pays for all of it, every operating hour.

Retro-commissioning (RCx) fixes sequences, not hardware. It is engineering labour, not capital, which is why it is usually the cheapest savings available on any plant. But unverified savings decay. Operators tweak, schedules drift, "temporary" overrides become permanent. Measurement and verification with plant-level submetering — chillers, pumps, air handling units — is what turns a claimed saving into a reportable one. The IPMVP framework (EVO 10000-1) exists precisely for this: verify, then claim.

Codes keep raising the floor beneath all of it. ASHRAE 90.1 lifts minimum design efficiency on the new-build side; in Asia, Hong Kong's Buildings Energy Efficiency Ordinance (Cap. 610), Singapore's Green Mark scheme and comparable frameworks push existing stock the same direction. Meanwhile refrigerant phase-outs increasingly force the replacement decision on ageing plant regardless of its condition — the R22 exit is behind most markets, and the R410A transition is already reshaping procurement lists.

A defensible pathway, in order:

1. Retro-commission before any capital submission — re-baseline what the plant actually delivers today.
2. Submeter and verify (M&V) — so every subsequent claim carries data, including the ESG report [IPMVP, EVO 10000-1].
3. Replace on lifecycle evidence — remaining useful life, refrigerant availability, code floor — not on age alone.

The sequence matters because it protects the capital decision: equipment chosen against measured loads and verified baselines is sized and specified better than equipment chosen against a malfunctioning status quo.
